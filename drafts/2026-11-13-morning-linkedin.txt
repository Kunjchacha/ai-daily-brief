A new AI paper is getting attention: Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits

The authors introduce Tree-Ising, a method that casts repairing a language model’s BF16 weights as a sparse discrete optimization problem solved via independent QUBO (Ising) formulations on a GPU. They report that Tree-Ising improves math benchmark performance, achieving 54.6% on MATH-500 (and 55.1% with simulated bifurcation), modifies fewer weights, requires only four forward-pass requests, and costs about $0.90 in GPU time.

The authors report: "On the agent's two layers, Tree-Ising achieves 54.6% on MATH-500, outperforming the agent's edit (51.7%) under the same evaluation protocol, while the base model scores 7.9% as reported."

Takeaway: These are the authors' own claims, and a preprint may not have been peer reviewed yet.
