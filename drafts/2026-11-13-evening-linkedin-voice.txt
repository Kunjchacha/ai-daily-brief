OpenAI has committed $2 million to research on AI and children's safety.

For a company of OpenAI's size, that is not a big number. What caught my attention is where the money is meant to go.

It isn't another set of guidelines. It is aimed at shared tools to measure the risks and the opportunities AI brings for young people.

That matters more than it sounds. Most of today's debate about children and AI runs on individual stories: a worrying chatbot conversation here, a promising tutoring trial there. Without a common way to measure what is actually happening, parents, schools and regulators end up arguing from different evidence.

Shared measurement could change that. If researchers use the same yardsticks, results can be compared, repeated and challenged. That is how a topic moves from opinion to evidence.

The commitment sits alongside a wider €8 million joint pledge from AI developers and safety organisations, made at the child safety summit in Paris.

Two things I'll be watching:
• whether independent researchers, not only the AI companies, get to use and shape these tools
• whether the findings are published, including the uncomfortable ones

$2 million won't settle the question. But measuring the problem properly is the right place to start.
