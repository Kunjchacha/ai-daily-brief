A new photo app wants to fix the shot itself, not just edit it afterwards.

Phota Labs, founded by two people who met at Adobe and worked on Lightroom and Photoshop, has launched a free iOS app it calls a personal AI photo assistant. TechCrunch reports it can turn a wide-angle group photo into a tighter portrait, or a selfie into what looks like a regular shot, while keeping people looking like themselves.

What caught my attention is not the editing. It is the two promises underneath it.

The first is personalisation. The founders say the app learns people's identity so that faces stay true. That is what makes the results believable, and it is also a model of what you and your family look like. The article doesn't say how that data is stored or whether processing happens on the phone or in the cloud.

The second is honesty. Shared images will carry invisible watermarks identifying them as altered by AI. That is a sensible default, and more consumer tools should do the same.

Two things I'd want to know before using it:
• where the identity model lives, and whether I can delete it
• whether the watermark survives screenshots and re-uploads

Better photos of the same moment is an appealing idea. Trust will decide whether people hand over their camera rolls to get them.

Would you let an app learn your face to get better photos?
